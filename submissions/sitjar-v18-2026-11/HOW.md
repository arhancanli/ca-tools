Construction: three methods, named per array in claims.csv. (1) CA(3516; 4, 14, 6) and CA(3786; 4, 15, 6): the 6 constant rows and the orbits of three starter rows under PGL(2, 5) on the symbols (points of PG(1, 5)) times a cyclic shift of the first k - 1 columns, one fixed column. (2) CA(6254; 4, 29, 6): delete a run and repair, from the first 29 columns of a wider array. (3) The other 43: classic post-optimisation (Nayeri, Colbourn and Konjevod 2013), repeated, of the record array of this table at each parameter set or at a larger k with columns deleted.
Built from: (1) nothing. (2) the first 29 columns of our CA(6446; 4, 37, 6) (arrays/CA_6446_4_37_6.txt of the release), an intermediate array of the chain that gave CA(6445; 4, 37, 6) from table/arrays CA_7199_4_37_6 (Bai XunDi, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23787055). (3) the record array of this table named in the construction of each claim: for 25 claims an array of Bai XunDi (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23787055) or, for CA(416; 3, 11, 6), the CA(418; 3, 11, 6) of Grey (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23877721); for the other 18 our earlier entry in this table, itself a post-optimisation of an array of Bai XunDi for all but CA(N; 4, 24, 15), CA(N; 4, 38, 6) and CA(N; 4, 39, 6).
Program: https://github.com/tns-research/covering-array-records release v1.8 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.24060956). (1) generators/v1.8/family-c/: "python3 run.py C4 14 10800 101" and "python3 run.py C4 15 10800 102" (run.py compiles starter.c with gcc -O3 and develops the starters with common.py); the starters found are in C4_CA_3516_4_14_6_starters.json and C4_CA_3786_4_15_6_starters.json, and common.py rebuilds the exact arrays from them. (2) generators/CA_6254_4_29_6.c, "gcc -O3 -std=gnu11 -o gen CA_6254_4_29_6.c -lm; cut -d' ' -f1-29 arrays/CA_6446_4_37_6.txt > start.txt; BEST_CANDIDATE=start.txt ./gen > out.txt" (arrays/CA_6446_4_37_6.txt is in the same release). (3) generators/postop.c, "gcc -O3 -march=native -o postop postop.c -lm; ./postop CA_<N>_<t>_<k>_<v>.txt t v <seconds> out.txt" with passes of 300, 1800 and 7200 seconds, each repeated on its own output. The programs of (1) and (2) were written by Claude Opus (Anthropic); no LLM runs in them. The searches are randomised and time-limited, so a rerun gives a valid array of similar size.
