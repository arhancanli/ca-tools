Construction: five methods, named per array in claims.csv. (1) CA(N; 4, k, 6) for k = 12, 16 and 21 to 25: the 6 constant rows and the orbits of three starter rows under PGL(2, 5) on the symbols (points of PG(1, 5)) times a cyclic shift of the first k - f columns, f fixed columns. (2) CA(6445; 4, 37, 6): delete a run and repair (uniquely covered 4-tuples moved first, then ejection chains). (3) CA(7350; 4, 38, 6) and CA(7783; 4, 39, 6): one column added to the best array for k - 1 by local search on the new column and greedy rows, then delete and repair. (4) CA(1807; 3, 13, 10), CA(2964; 3, 15, 12), CA(3510; 4, 13, 6): delete and repair, or a search over rows invariant under a cyclic shift of the columns, from our earlier arrays. (5) The other 22: classic post-optimisation (Nayeri, Colbourn and Konjevod 2013) of the record arrays of this table.
Built from: (1) nothing. (2) table/arrays CA_7199_4_37_6 (Bai XunDi, doi:10.5281/zenodo.23787055) through a chain of our intermediate arrays 7027, 6830, ..., 6446. (3) our CA(6830; 4, 37, 6), then CA(7350; 4, 38, 6). (4) our CA(1813; 3, 13, 10), CA(3057; 3, 15, 12) (both from arrays of Bai XunDi) and CA(3606; 4, 13, 6), all in this table. (5) the record array of this table at each parameter set, or at the larger k named in claims.csv with columns deleted; most are from Bai XunDi (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23787055).
Program: https://github.com/tns-research/covering-array-records release v1.7 (doi:10.5281/zenodo.23959429). (1) generators/v1.7/family/c4.py and c5.py (Python, NumPy), run as "python3 c5.py" with C5_SECONDS=4200, seed 12345 in common.py. (2) generators/CA_6445_4_37_6.c, "gcc -O3 -std=gnu11 -o gen CA_6445_4_37_6.c -lm; BEST_CANDIDATE=CA_6446_4_37_6.txt ./gen > out.txt". (3) generators/v1.7/extend/chain.py start_37.txt 38 39 ext_seconds 720 (extend.c and gen.c in the same folder). (4) generators/CA_1807_3_13_10.c, CA_2964_3_15_12.c, CA_3510_4_13_6.c, each with BEST_CANDIDATE set to the starting array. (5) generators/postop.c, "gcc -O3 -march=native -o postop postop.c -lm; ./postop CA_<N>_<t>_<k>_<v>.txt t v 300 out.txt", repeated on its own output. The programs of (1), (2) and (4) were written by Claude Opus (Anthropic) in an automated search loop; no LLM runs in them. The searches are randomised and time-limited, so a rerun gives a valid array of similar size.
